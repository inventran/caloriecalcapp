By CalorieCalc Nutrition Team • Updated October 2026 • 🛒 USDA & Eurobarometer Retail Protein Analysis

Hitting 130 to 170 grams of protein per day while staying inside a 1,500–1,900 kcal calorie deficit is nearly impossible if your cart is filled with "high-protein" marketing traps like peanut butter, trail mix, or standard cheddar cheese. Those foods provide protein, but at a steep caloric price tag of 180 to 250 calories for every 7 grams of protein.

The 3 Supermarket Rules for a Calorie Deficit
  • The 10-for-100 Protein Rule: Add a zero to the protein grams on the nutrition label. If that number is close to or higher than total calories (at least 10 g protein per 100 kcal), it is a true lean protein anchor.
  • Compare Cost Per 30g Protein (Not Price Per Pound/Kilo): A $3.99 box of "protein granola" often provides only 40 g of protein in the entire box ($3.00 per 30 g protein and 450 kcal), whereas a $4.99 tub of 0% Greek yogurt or Skyr provides 90 g of protein ($1.66 per 30 g protein and 160 kcal).
  • Stock Frozen Produce & Berries: Frozen broccoli florets, spinach, green beans, and mixed berries cost 40% less than fresh, never spoil mid-week, and let you add 300 g (10.5 oz) of volume to dinner for under 90 calories.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is canned tuna safe to eat every day on a budget?

Choose skipjack ("chunk light") tuna in water rather than albacore ("white") tuna, as skipjack contains roughly one-third the mercury of larger albacore tuna. Most food safety agencies (FDA and EFSA) recommend limiting canned tuna to 2–3 cans per week and rotating with chicken breast, eggs, sardines, cod, or Skyr/Greek yogurt.

Why are boiled or baked potatoes recommended on a calorie deficit grocery list?

On the Holt Satiety Index, plain boiled or air-fried potatoes scored higher for fullness per calorie than any other tested food—more than 3 times as filling as croissants or white bread. A 200 g (7 oz) potato contains only 154 calories, 0 g fat, and 4 g fiber.
