How to Count Calories in Home-Cooked Meals (Plus Raw vs. Cooked Weight Conversion Chart)

By CalorieCalc Product & Nutrition Research Team • 11 min read • Data: USDA FoodData Central • Updated October 2026

Weighing 6 oz (170g) of cooked chicken breast and logging it under a standard "raw chicken breast" entry undercounts your meal by 68 calories and 13 grams of protein. Conversely, logging 200g (7 oz) of steamed white rice using a dry/uncooked entry overestimates your intake by nearly 460 calories.

Whether you are portioning out a single plate from a family casserole or batch-prepping 5 lunch containers for the workweek, accurate home-cooked logging comes down to two things: matching your weighed state (raw vs. cooked) and dividing by the total cooked pot weight.

1. Master Raw vs. Cooked Weight Conversion Chart (US oz & Metric g)

During cooking, meats, poultry, and fish lose 20% to 28% of their weight as water evaporates, concentrating calories into a smaller mass. Conversely, dry grains, pasta, and legumes absorb water and expand 2.3x to 3.5x by weight.

2. The 3-Step Multi-Serving Batch Recipe Formula

When cooking a multi-ingredient dish like turkey chili, lasagna, or chicken curry for multiple days, never guess what "one ladle" equals. Use the Calorie-Per-Gram (or Ounce) Method:

Step 1: Total Recipe Calories = Sum of all raw ingredients + cooking oil
Step 2: Calorie Density = Total Recipe Calories ÷ Final Cooked Dish Weight (in g or oz)
Step 3: Your Bowl Calories = Weight of Your Serving × Calorie Density

Practical Example: You cook a batch of turkey chili totaling 2,400 calories of raw ingredients. After simmering, the entire pot of chili (minus the empty pot's weight) weighs 1,200 grams (42.3 oz). That equals exactly 2.0 calories per gram (56.7 kcal per oz). When you scoop a 350g (12.3 oz) bowl for dinner, your serving is 700 calories.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much raw chicken breast is 4 oz (113g) or 150g cooked?

Boneless, skinless chicken breast loses roughly 25% of its weight as water during cooking. Therefore, 4 oz (113g) of cooked chicken breast equals 5.3 oz (151g) of raw chicken (181 kcal, 34g protein), and 150g of cooked chicken equals 200g raw (240 kcal, 45g protein).

Do I need to weigh non-starchy vegetables like lettuce or cucumbers?

Leafy greens and watery vegetables (cucumbers, celery, spinach) have very low caloric density (10–20 calories per cup / 100g). While weighing cooking oils, meats, and starches is important, estimating leafy greens rarely affects your weight loss trajectory.
